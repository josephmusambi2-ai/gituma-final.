About us

A Kenyan non-profit empowering communities, sharing love and driving sustainable livelihoods and wellness, with diabetes and NCD care at its heart. Headquartered in Kasarani, we serve communities across Kenya. We began in Buuri Constituency, Meru County, and that is where our model was tested.

Our story

The Gituma Makathimo Foundation is a non-profit, started out of the wish to give smiles of hope to people.

The initiative began after our chairman, a diabetes patient himself, saw the need to educate, raise awareness and help others living with the disease. Many people with diabetes die without knowing they have it, eat the wrong foods, or cannot reach medical treatment because of poverty. That is the big reason behind the foundation.

Our mission is to inspire joy and smiles in communities.

Who founded it

Gituma Makathimo, a well-known local media personality and long-serving Head of Radio at Mediamax Network's Meru FM. The trust he has built with listeners is the foundation of this work.

Our pledge

To serve all Kenyans in equal measure, including remote communities that centralized systems overlook.

Our vision

A healthy, compassionate and self-sustaining society where youth, families and vulnerable individuals have the wellness, skills and opportunities to lead dignified lives. That means self-sustaining, youth-driven local economies across Kenya.

Our mission

To extend love and practical support to communities through targeted health interventions, diabetes care, technical skilling, sustainable livelihoods and strategic partnerships. We empower young people through structured sports scouting, grassroots economic mapping and strategic media advocacy, and at the heart of it all we aim to inspire joy and smiles in communities.

Organizational overview

The Gituma Makathimo Foundation is a Kenyan non-profit dedicated to holistic community transformation, rooted in sharing love through active service.

By joining economic skilling with health interventions, especially against the rising burden of diabetes and other non-communicable diseases (NCDs), the foundation bridges socio-economic and health gaps across vulnerable populations. It restores dignity, fosters self-reliance and builds healthier, more resilient households.

The localized key

Many programs use one model everywhere. We start with what a community already does well, such as its self-help groups, youth groups and women's chamas, and put resources directly into them.

Projects follow the local land. In drier lowlands, as in Buuri: bee farming, poultry and cotton. In the upper, water-rich zones: specialty orchards and aquaculture.

What we believe

Every community is different

A plan that works in a dry lowland will not work in a water-rich highland. We start by listening, then design with residents.

Equal service

We serve all Kenyans in equal measure, including remote communities that centralized systems overlook.

Local wealth, not dependency

General aid can create dependency, while local infrastructure builds wealth that stays in the community.

Accountability

Programs

Our four core pillars of intervention, our sport and media programs, and five community ventures.

Core pillars of intervention

Four areas where we combine care, skills and livelihoods, so health and income lift each other.

1. Compassionate outreach and social welfare ("Sharing Love")

Holistic family support: emotional, material and social care for vulnerable, bereaved and low-income households.
Community wellness drives: love-in-action medical camps, food distribution and psycho-social support networks that leave no family behind.

2. Diabetes and NCD awareness, prevention and care

Screening and early detection: regular, free community camps for blood glucose and hypertension in rural and urban settlements.
Nutrition and lifestyle education: healthy living, active lifestyles and traditional, nutrient-dense crop value chains to prevent and manage Type 2 diabetes.
Patient support networks: community support groups for people and families living with diabetes, for treatment adherence, peer mentorship and affordable access to care.

3. Youth empowerment and vocational skilling

We equip out-of-school youth with practical technical skills in electrical work, digital skills, green technologies and modern trades. We then link trainees to official TVETA certification and starter toolkits, so they can set up viable micro-enterprises.

4. Sustainable agribusiness and livelihood resilience

We promote climate-smart agriculture and nutrition-focused crop production, such as indigenous vegetables and high-fiber grains, that directly support diabetes management and food security.

More ways we serve

Alongside the four core pillars, we run sport, enterprise, health and media programs that open doors for young people.

Youth talent and sports

Our flagship is the Gituma Makathimo Foundation Football Tournament League, a talent pipeline for grassroots players, starting in Meru and open to every county. Through sports management networks such as footedgeAfrica, players get a path to national and international clubs, and young people get meaningful engagement instead of idleness and substance abuse.

Agribusiness and micro-enterprise

Community-level incubators train youth groups in high-yield, climate-resilient farming, cooperative management and adding value to local produce.

Public health and social welfare

Medical camps, nutrition drives and preventive health awareness, including diabetes and lifestyle disease screening, in underserved rural areas. Diabetes awareness is where the foundation began.

Media advocacy and public mobilization

Using radio and communications experience to give communities a platform, hold decisions to account, rally corporate sponsors, and make sure policy-makers hear residents.

Our three core objectives

These commitments run through everything we do.

Diabetes awareness

We hold regular, planned medical camps with partner organizations so people live healthily and avoid this lifestyle disease. We also encourage regular check-ups and teach healthy feeding habits.

Talent development for healthy living (Talanta na Uhai)

We nurture talents such as football, all geared towards living and staying healthy, and keeping the young generation away from drug and substance abuse.

Masomo Kijijini

We promote education in villages by helping young people with fees, uniforms and better living conditions. We visit schools and support them with book donations and the construction of classrooms, latrines and more.

Five ventures, matched to the land

We fund, train and monitor each one. Which ventures a community runs is its own decision, made at a public meeting and by vote.

Community bee farming

Modern hives and processing equipment go to youth groups in semi-arid zones. Honey is sold locally, so the group earns income quickly. Suited to drier lowlands.

Cotton and cash crop revival

Pest-resistant seed is distributed with agricultural extension officers, so cotton fields can support family incomes again. Suited to drier lowlands.

Fruit tree orchards

Dual-purpose fruit trees in public schools fight soil erosion, give shade and feed students, while children learn about nutrition and conservation. Suited to upper, water-rich zones.

Micro-poultry cooperatives

Women's groups run poultry cooperatives with starter feed and vaccine schedules, selling eggs to regional markets. Suited to drier lowlands.

Sustainable inland fish farming

Community-managed earth ponds give steady protein and income that does not depend on rain. Suited to upper, water-rich zones.

How we work

Residents decide. We support and stay accountable.

  1. IdentifyThe team visits a ward or community, such as Timau or Ruiri in Buuri, and starts with a public community meeting.
  2. Audit resourcesWe list existing projects to see where support can stop them being abandoned before starting new ones.
  3. Residents chooseThe community votes on the ventures it wants, for example water harvesting before poultry.
  4. Deliver and overseeWe supply seedlings, sports gear or hives, track progress, and set up community committees to run things long term.

Our journey

July 2021: Environment and water alliance

With environmental expert Wakili Francis Mburu Muchua, we began planting climate-resilient fruit trees and building water harvesting systems in public and private schools across Buuri, tackling food insecurity while teaching children about nutrition and conservation.

September 2021: Four-pillar community plan

The baseline strategy was presented to regional community forums, setting the "Every door has its own key" rule.

2022 to 2025: Media and community mobilization

Radio programming at Meru FM and cultural events such as the Tharaka Nithi Uragate Cultural Festival were used for public interest drives and to voice local grievances.

2025 to 2026: Youth sports strategy

Links with soccer networks, including footedgeAfrica, led to grassroots tournaments in wards like Ruiri/Rwarera, reaching youth outside the school cycle.

Our care model: from screening to livelihood

Health and income are connected, so one family can move through all four steps with us.

  1. ScreenFree community camps check blood glucose and blood pressure, in rural and urban settlements.
  2. EducateHealthy eating, active living and nutrient-dense traditional crops to prevent and manage Type 2 diabetes.
  3. SupportCommunity support groups give peer mentorship, help with treatment adherence and routes to affordable care.
  4. Skill upTechnical skills, TVETA certification, starter toolkits and climate-smart farming build steady income.

Common questions

Who is the foundation for?

All Kenyans. We began in Buuri, Meru County, and our approach is open to any community.

Who decides what a community gets?

Residents do. We hold a public meeting, review existing projects and the community votes.

How can I give?

By M-Pesa or bank transfer. See the Donate page.

Can I sponsor something specific?

Yes: a football team, a community incubator or a health camp. Contact us to arrange it.

Can my group bring a project?

Yes. Tell us your ward, your group and what you want to do.

Are the health screenings free?

Yes. We host regular, free community screening camps for blood glucose and hypertension. Contact us to find the next camp near you.

What skills do you teach young people?

Practical technical skills such as electrical work, digital skills, green technologies and modern trades, linked to TVETA certification and starter toolkits.

Do I need qualifications to volunteer?

Some roles, such as health work, need them. Others just need time and willingness. Tell us what you can offer.
